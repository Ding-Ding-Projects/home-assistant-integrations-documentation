TTC transit

TTC transit

Reads the Toronto Transit Commission's public information and shows current delays, next departures, elevators and escalators out of service, and vehicles near a stop. It adds entities, automation triggers and conditions, a page, eight dashboard cards and a Transit dashboard.

State. The integration is written and its tests pass against saved public answers. The page, the cards, the dashboard and the automation editors are written and type-checked, but nothing has been seen in a browser or in a real build yet, and nothing has run against the live feeds beyond a handful of requests made to learn their shape.

Contains information licensed under the Open Government Licence - Toronto. Every entity carries this attribution, and the page and cards show it.

What it reads

Feed Address Used for How often
Live service alerts, the feed behind the agency's own alerts page https://alerts.ttc.ca/api/alerts/live-alerts Route alerts, elevator and escalator outages, general alerts Once a minute for alerts. Every 3 minutes for elevators and escalators, reusing a recent alerts answer so the feed is asked once
Next vehicle arrival predictions https://retro.umoiq.com/service/publicJSONFeed with command=predictions&a=ttc&stopId=<stop> Next departures at the chosen stops Every 45 seconds, one request per chosen stop
The same service, command=vehicleLocations&a=ttc&r=<route> Vehicles near a stop Every 25 seconds, only while a card or the page is showing them. Never when nobody is looking
The same service, command=routeList and command=routeConfig Route names, the stops of a route, and stop positions Route names once a day. Stops while choosing them. Stop positions once per route while vehicles are shown

No key and no account are needed.

What is sent

Only the chosen stop numbers and route numbers, and the agency code ttc. Nothing about the home is sent: no location, no address, no identifier. The requests name the project in their user agent and carry no contact details. No request is made at all when nothing is being watched.

Polite polling

  • Each request has a 20 second timeout.
  • After an error the wait doubles each time, up to 15 minutes. A refusal (403, 429 or 503) waits at least as long as the feed asks.
  • The next attempt time and the last error are shown on the "information is fresh" entities and on the page.

Old information

Every figure carries the time it was read (read_at). When the information is older than its limit, the entities become unavailable rather than showing old figures as current. The limits are 10 minutes for alerts, 20 minutes for elevators and escalators, 3 minutes for departures and 2 minutes for vehicle positions. The page and cards dim old figures and say since when the TTC has not answered.

Setting it up

Three steps:

  1. What to watch. Choose any of service alerts, elevators and escalators, and next departures.
  2. Routes and stations. Subway lines, buses and streetcars are chosen from the real route list by search. If the list cannot be read, the subway lines are still offered. Stations are chosen from a list of rapid transit stations; a station that is not listed can be typed.
  3. Stops for next departures. Choose from the stops of the chosen bus and streetcar routes, or type the number from the stop sign. Each stop is checked against the predictions service before it is accepted.

Options change routes, stations and stops. Reconfigure changes what is watched. Only one entry can exist.

What the agency does not offer

  • Next train times for the subway. The predictions service covers buses and streetcars only. Asking it for a subway route returns an error, and no public feed without a key for subway next train times was found on the agency's developer and open data pages. Typing a subway station as a stop explains this rather than failing silently.
  • How many elevators a station has. The alerts feed lists only the elevators that are out, so the step-free card shows the elevators that are out and the place each one cuts off, not "3 of 4 working".
  • When an outage ends. The alerts feed has no estimate for elevators and escalators, so none is shown.

Entities

Entity Kind One per State and attributes
Status Sensor, enum Route regular, slow, delay, detour, reduced_service, no_service. The alerts in effect, with the agency's own text, are in alerts
Problem Binary sensor, problem Route On when the status is not regular
Next departure, Second departure, Third departure Sensor, timestamp Stop The predicted time; route, direction, vehicle and predicted: true in the attributes
Minutes to next departure Sensor, duration Stop Whole minutes until the next predicted departure
Elevators out, Escalators out Sensor Station A count; the outages with the agency's text in outages
Step-free Binary sensor Station On when no elevator is reported out. The feed cannot say whether a station is accessible at all
Alerts Sensor Network The number of alerts in effect, with counts by kind
New alert Event Network Fires once for each alert that was not there before. Event types: route, elevator, escalator, general
Service alerts are fresh, Elevator information is fresh, Departures are fresh Binary sensor, diagnostic Network On while that feed's information is recent enough. Last error, failures and next attempt are in the attributes

There is one device per route, per stop, per station and for the network. Each device links to the TTC transit page.

Automation triggers

Each trigger compares an answer with the previous one, so it fires once for each thing that happened, not on every refresh. What is already true when the automation starts does not fire.

Trigger Fires
Route gets a delay (ttc.route_delayed) When a route in regular service gets any problem. For one route, or for every chosen route
Route is back to regular service (ttc.route_resumed) When a route with a problem is back to regular
Next departure is within (ttc.departure_within) Once for each departure, when its predicted time first drops to the chosen number of minutes or less
Time to leave (ttc.time_to_leave) Once for each departure, when it is time to walk to the stop; leave_by is in the trigger data
Elevator or escalator goes out or comes back (ttc.lift_changed) Once for each outage that starts, ends, or both, at one station or any
New alert is posted (ttc.new_alert) Once for each new alert, optionally of one kind

Conditions: Route has a problem (ttc.route_has_problem) and Station is step-free (ttc.station_is_step_free). Old or missing information does not pass either condition.

The automation editor has tailored controls for each trigger, condition and action.

Actions

  • ttc.refresh reads the feeds again now.
  • ttc.get_departures returns the predicted departures at any stop number, read now. It accepts an optional route and limit.

The page

Settings, TTC transit. It has four sections: Now, Alerts, Lifts and Stops, with search in the top bar. All times use the 24-hour clock. The "Add the transit dashboard" button creates a dashboard that uses the Transit dashboard strategy.

Dashboard cards

Card Shows
ttc-alert-banner The most serious alert on your routes, as a banner
ttc-subway The status of each chosen subway line
ttc-departures Predicted departures at a stop, by route and direction
ttc-leave-by When to leave to catch the next departure, with a walking time
ttc-route-alerts Alerts on your routes, in the agency's own words
ttc-step-free Elevators and escalators out at your stations, and "Not step-free to ..." when the agency's text names the place an elevator cuts off
ttc-vehicles Vehicles of the stop's routes within 2.5 km, on a schematic strip, with "Positions as reported N seconds ago". Tapping opens the app's own map with the stop and the vehicles. The card asks for positions only while it is showing
ttc-line-history A route's last 24 hours, drawn from the recorded status sensor, with the time spent in each status

Each card has a visual editor. The Transit dashboard, offered in the new dashboard dialog, builds these cards each time it opens from the routes, stops and stations the integration watches, so it follows changes made in the integration's options.

Verification

  • Core tests in core/tests/components/ttc/. They use saved public answers and never contact the network. They cover the setup flow, the coordinators (timeouts, server errors, refusals, malformed answers, going stale and recovering), every entity kind, triggers firing once, conditions, actions, page data, vehicle positions only while subscribed, and diagnostics.
  • Not yet verified: anything in a browser, the cards, the dashboard, the device page link, and behaviour against the live feeds over time.

Configuration details

In Home Assistant, open Settings → Devices & services, choose Add integration, and search for TTC transit.

The routes configuration step lists routes, stations.

The stops configuration step lists stops.

The user configuration step lists watch_alerts, watch_departures, watch_lifts.
